Mechanism guide

Two tracks, one moving bar

The lab is a weekly-tick simulation of one claim: skill compounds per shipped iteration, while "ready" drifts with the field. This page maps every control on the Lab tab to the mechanism it exercises.

Honest limits. Six knobs, no randomness, no labor market. The model proves a mechanism — that deferral is expensive when the target moves — not a prediction about your career.

Start here

What this demo proves

Press Readiness treadmill: the waiter's study bar rises every week and the amber bar-ticks rise faster. The READY caret exits the right edge of the ledger and the lane is stamped NEVER SHIPPED. Press Steady field: the same waiter ships at week 34 — late, seventeen iterations behind the starter, but on the board.

The mechanism

Controls and what they prove

Track 01

The Starter

Ships v0 at week 0, then one iteration every iterWeeks. Each ship adds learnPerIter skill — a step line that clears the bar around week 16 on defaults.

Proves
readiness is an output of shipping — the starter crosses the bar by shipping, not before.
Key detail
skill = learnPerIter × ships so far; indigo squares mark each ship week.
Track 02

The Waiter

Banks studyPerWeek of prep and may ship only when study clears the bar for that week. Prep converts to skill on the ship week; after that, iterations compound identically.

Proves
study alone never lands on the ledger — it only counts once it ships.
Key detail
study × week ≥ barStart + drift × week gates the first ship.
Slider 01

Field drift

How fast "ready" moves, in skill-units per week — framework releases, new eval expectations, deprecated APIs.

Proves
at drift ≥ study the inequality can never hold: this is the treadmill.
Key detail
drag it past the study rate and the caret leaves the frame.
Slider 02

Study rate

What the waiter banks in prep per week — courses, reading, roadmap maintenance.

Proves
even generous study only delays the ship week; it never buys compounding.
Key detail
the ship week solves w* = barStart / (study − drift).
Slider 03

Iteration cadence

Weeks between shipped iterations once a track is shipping. Smaller is faster compounding.

Proves
at 6-week cadence the waiter ships late and still finishes ahead — cadence is the whole game.
Key detail
try the Slow iteration preset: verdict flips to caught-up.
Slider 04

Runway

Weeks left on the calendar — the deadline column stamped 2027 at the right edge.

Proves
shorten it to 20 weeks and the waiter's projected ready week lands past the deadline.
Key detail
Compressed runway reproduces exactly that.

Run locally

Inspect the evidence

Try node scripts/track.mjs --scenario readiness-treadmill to watch the waiter study all 64 weeks and ship nothing.

Limits

What this demo does not claim

  • Skill and "ready" are abstract units — real readiness is messier and partly unmeasurable.
  • Drift is a constant rate; real fields lurch, plateau, and sometimes slow down.
  • The waiter stops gaining from study at the ship week — a modeling choice that makes the mechanism legible, not a law.
  • Iteration gain is linear; real compounding depends on feedback quality, not just cadence.
  • A deadline of 64 weeks is illustrative — your runway is your own.
